# 16. Places to Visit

A running list of destinations for the truck, and later the truck with a trailer. Add a place to the **wish list** when it comes up. Move it to **visited** after the trip, with anything worth knowing for next time.

## Rig limits for route and site planning

Check a destination and its approach roads against these figures before committing to it.

| Limit | Truck alone | With a fifth wheel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Height | **82.5 in (6 ft 10.5 in)**, measured ([chapter 1](01-vehicle-information.md)) | Set by the trailer. The toy haulers screened so far run **12 ft 0 in to 13 ft 5 in** tall ([chapter 15](15-fifth-wheel-candidates.md)); measure the chosen trailer with its A/C |
| Length | **22 ft 2 in** bumper to bumper ([chapter 1](01-vehicle-information.md)) | Trailer capped at **35 ft** ([chapter 15](15-fifth-wheel-candidates.md)); measure the combined length once hitched |
| Weight | 11,900 lb GVWR | See [chapter 10](10-fifth-wheel-and-towing.md) for combined ratings and scale weights |

What to check for each place:

- **Campground site length** against the combined length, plus room to swing in. Many state and national park sites were built for shorter rigs.
- **Low clearances** on the route: bridges, tunnels, fuel station canopies and drive-throughs.
- **Road restrictions**: grade, switchbacks, and vehicle-length limits on scenic roads. Some park roads ban vehicles over a set length.
- **Fuel stops** on remote stretches, especially when towing, when range drops.

## Trailer length and state parks - 35 ft against ~40 ft

Added 26 September 2026. The extra 5 ft is a large step down in what can be booked: 35 ft is about where most state park sites top out, and above that the choice narrows sharply.

**How much each fits.** No national dataset exists for state parks. The best published figure is for national park campgrounds ([Camper Report](https://camperreport.com/best-rv-length-for-national-parks/)):

| Trailer length | National park campgrounds that fit it |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 35 ft | **73%** |
| Up to 40 ft | **53%** |

State parks follow the same pattern and are often stricter. Many older loops cap sites at **30-35 ft**; newer or renovated loops and full-hookup pull-throughs are where the long sites are ([America's State Parks](https://www.americasstateparks.org/rv-sizes-at-state-parks/)).

**Check what a published length measures.** Reservation systems ask for the trailer's **overall** length, front cap to rear bumper. Fifth-wheel specs are often **hitch to rear** - Grand Design's are - so the front cap over the truck bed adds roughly **2-3 ft**. A Momentum 350G listed at 39 ft 9 in is about **42 ft overall** on a reservation form, and a trailer listed at 35 ft pin-to-rear is about 37-38 ft. Confirm or measure before booking.

**Combined length with this truck.** Front bumper to kingpin is about **17 ft 8 in** (176 in wheelbase plus ~40 in front overhang, less a few inches for the kingpin ahead of the rear axle):

| Trailer, pin to rear | Truck front to trailer rear |
| --- | --- |
| 35 ft | **~53 ft** |
| 39 ft 11 in | **~58 ft** |

This matters on back-in sites where the truck must fit on the pad, and on tight loop roads. Many sites expect the truck parked beside the trailer or in a separate spot, so the pad usually needs to cover only the trailer.

**In practice:**

- **35 ft** books most modern state park sites and many older ones, with more choice on holiday weekends.
- **~40 ft (about 42 ft overall)** means fewer parks and sites, booked earlier. Read each campground's site list for its maximum equipment length rather than the park-wide limit, and watch for tight loops, trees and low limbs - several candidates in [chapter 15](15-fifth-wheel-candidates.md) are 13 ft 2 in to 13 ft 6 in tall.
- **Filtering:** ReserveAmerica, the state reservation portals and recreation.gov filter by equipment length. Reviews on The Dyrt and Campendium often flag sites that are longer or shorter than listed.

This is the trade-off behind the **35 ft hard limit** and the separate **40 ft list** in chapter 15.

## Trip costs - propane

Added 26 September 2026. Prices are for refilling the trailer's own cylinders at a pay-at-the-pump station (U-Haul, Tractor Supply and similar), **excluding the western states**.

| Region | Refill price |
| --- | --- |
| South and Midwest | ~$3.00-3.50/gal |
| Northeast | ~$3.75-4.00/gal, some stores up to ~$4.50 |
| **Planning figure** | **~$3.50/gal** |

**Refill, do not exchange.** An exchange "20 lb" cylinder (Blue Rhino and similar) holds only 15 lb, so it costs far more per gallon than refilling your own. Residential delivery prices are lower (EIA national average **$2.67/gal**, end of March 2026) and do not apply to cylinder refills.

**Cost for the trailer's two 30 lb cylinders** (about 7 gal each, ~14 gal together - the 60 lb propane allowance on the trailer labels in [chapter 15](15-fifth-wheel-candidates.md)), refilled from empty every two weeks:

| | At $3.00/gal | At $3.50/gal | At $4.00/gal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per refill, both cylinders | $42 | **$49** | $56 |
| Per month (~2.2 refills) | $91 | **$106** | $121 |
| Per year (26 refills) | $1,092 | **$1,274** | $1,456 |

**Budget about $50 every two weeks, roughly $1,300 a year.** Actual spending is usually lower: cylinders are topped up before they run dry, and only what goes in is paid for. **The furnace is the big user** - in cold weather it can empty both cylinders in a week or less, while mild weather (fridge, water heater, cooktop) can stretch 14 gal over several weeks. A single 20 lb cylinder (~4.7 gal) costs ~$14-19 to refill.

Sources: [PropaneTankRefillCost](https://propanetankrefillcost.com/), [U-Haul refill 2026](https://propanetankrefillcost.com/u-haul/), [Tractor Supply](https://www.tractorsupply.com/tsc/services/propane-refill), [EIA residential propane price](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/hist/LeafHandler.ashx?n=PET&s=M_EPLLPA_PRS_NUS_DPG&f=M).

## Trip plan - Belleville, IL to Crestview, FL, towing a cargo trailer

Planned 7 October 2026. **Truck plus a 7,000 lb enclosed cargo trailer** (bumper pull), from ZIP 62226 to Crestview, FL 32539, **60 mph maximum**. Route and mileage from OSRM routing, town to town; times are at the 60 mph cap. All of it is in the **Central time zone**.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Distance | **~710 mi** |
| Driving time, non-stop | **~12.5-13.5 hr** |
| With fuel and rest stops | **~14-15 hr** door to door |
| Fuel at ~9 mpg (8-10) | **~79 gal** (71-89) |
| Fuel cost at $3.50/gal | **~$277** ($249-311) |
| Range, 48 gal tank at 9 mpg | ~430 mi to empty; **refuel by ~300 mi** so the tank never runs below a quarter |

**Fuel stops** (refuel early with a trailer - choose stations with **pull-through diesel or truck lanes**, or the outer pumps at large travel centers):

| Stop | Mile | From last fill | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ~217 | 217 mi, ~24 gal | **Clarksville, TN** (I-24 exits 4-11) - fuel before the Nashville loop |
| 2 | ~430-440 | ~220 mi, ~25 gal | **Athens or Cullman, AL** (I-65 exits 304-318) - large travel centers |
| Arrive | ~711 | ~275 mi, ~31 gal | Top up in Crestview for the return |

**Suggested timing.** Leave about **5:00 am**: Nashville ~10:30 am, past Birmingham before ~3:00 pm, Crestview ~7:00 pm. **That is a long day alone with a trailer** - for two days, stop overnight near **Cullman or Birmingham** (~440-480 mi), leaving ~230-270 mi for the second day.

**All-interstate alternative.** Stay on I-65 to Mobile, then I-10 East to Crestview (I-10 exit 56): no two-lane roads, but roughly 80-100 mi farther. Worth it in the dark or in bad weather.

**Before leaving**

- **Truck tires** 60 psi front / 80 psi rear, cold ([chapter 1](01-vehicle-information.md)); **trailer tires** at the cold pressure on their sidewall or the trailer's tire label; check lug nuts on both.
- **Load the trailer about 60% ahead of its axles** for **10-15% tongue weight - roughly 700-1,050 lb** on 7,000 lb. Strap everything down.
- **Brake controller gain** set with a test stop from 20-25 mph; check the **breakaway cable** and battery, all lights, crossed safety chains, coupler latched and pinned.
- **Weigh at a CAT scale** early (there are truck stops on I-64 near the start) and record it in the [scale weights log](../logs/scale-weights.md).
- Use **Tow/Haul** mode; on the I-65 downgrades let the transmission hold a gear rather than riding the brakes.
- Fill up before leaving so the 217-mile first leg starts on a full tank.
